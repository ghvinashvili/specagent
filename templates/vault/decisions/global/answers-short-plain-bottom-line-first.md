# Answers short, plain, bottom line first

**Rule.** The first sentence is the direct answer in plain words (yes / no / partly; does it work). Then at most one
small table and the caveat. A short question gets a short reply; detail is offered in one line ("I can list the
cases"), not written out. No "§3", "#28" or path shorthand: say the idea, and name a file only when the owner has to
open it. Methodology and control tables go to the report file on disk, not into the reply.

**Why.** The owner could not follow replies full of measurements, section numbers or a dozen narrow reports, and had to
ask what they meant; a bare count with no word on what it counted left them unable to make it out. They asked to be
talked to a bit less.

**How to apply.**
- *A question mid-work* is answered after the current tool call, before continuing, with the answer first; a question
  about doing something the owner proposed counts as consent (say when it runs). Never many tool calls without a
  visible line of what is happening.
- *Short never means bare.* When a number justifies a decision, first say in plain words what is measured (what is
  being counted, before the count), show the consequence with arithmetic the owner can follow, and say why the cheaper
  option is acceptable. That is the fact that changes what the owner will do next, which the short-reply rule allows;
  unrequested comparisons, edge cases and background stay out. Summaries are where the fact gets cut first, so check
  them for it.
- *Closing a long investigation.* Two or three sentences before any table: does it work, how much, the single thing
  still unknown. When the next measurement cannot change the decision, stop measuring.
- Rigour stays: never soften a negative result or claim an unmeasured number.
- When the owner cannot tell what a project holds, give a whole-picture map grouped by purpose before proposing a step.
